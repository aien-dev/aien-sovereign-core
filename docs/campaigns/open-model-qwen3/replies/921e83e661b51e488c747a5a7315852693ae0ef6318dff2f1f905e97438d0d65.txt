A savings account is a simple way to store money safely.  
When you deposit money into your savings account, it goes into a balance that grows over time.  
Deposits can be made in cash or through a bank transfer.  
The bank pays you interest on your balance, which means you earn money just for keeping your funds in the account.  
Interest is usually small but adds up over time, helping your savings grow.  
You can check your balance at any time through your bank app or online portal.  
Balances are updated automatically after every deposit or withdrawal.  
Withdrawing money from your savings account means taking funds out of your balance.  
You can withdraw money in cash or by writing a check.  
Withdrawals may have limits or require a fee, depending on the bank.  
Interest rates vary by bank and account type, so it's good to compare options.  
Balancing your savings helps you plan for future expenses or goals.  
You can set up automatic deposits to build your balance over time.  
Interest is typically calculated monthly and added to your balance.  
If you withdraw money, your balance decreases and interest on the remaining amount continues.  
The balance is always the total of all deposits minus all withdrawals.  
You can monitor your balance to avoid overdrawing or missing interest.  
Understanding deposits, withdrawals, interest, and balances helps you manage your money wisely.  
Savings accounts are a great first step toward building financial stability.
