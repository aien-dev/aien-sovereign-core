# Sharpening a Kitchen Knife by Hand

A sharp knife is safer than a dull one because it needs less force.
This guide uses a whetstone and a steady hand.

## Gather Your Tools
- A two-sided whetstone, coarse and fine
- A bowl of water to soak the stone
- A damp towel to stop the stone sliding
- The knife you want to sharpen

## Sharpen the Edge
Soak the whetstone for ten minutes until bubbles stop rising.
Hold the blade tilted like a triangle, about fifteen degrees, against the coarse side.
Push the edge across the stone from heel to tip in one smooth stroke.
Repeat ten strokes, then turn the knife over and match them on the other side.
Move to the fine side and repeat with lighter pressure.
Keep the blade angled the same for every stroke so the edge stays even.

## Test and Store
Slice a sheet of paper; a sharp edge cuts it cleanly.
If it tears, go back to the fine side for a few more strokes.
Rinse and dry the blade before you put it away.
Store the knife in a block or on a magnetic strip.
Never leave it loose in a drawer with other tools.
Let the whetstone dry fully before you store it too.
