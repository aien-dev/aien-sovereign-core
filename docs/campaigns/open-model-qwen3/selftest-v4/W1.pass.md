# Home composting for beginners

Composting turns kitchen and garden waste into dark, crumbly soil.
It needs very little money and only a little attention each week.

## Choosing a bin

A closed plastic bin keeps the heap warm and tidy.
A wooden box with slats lets more air in.
Pick a spot with partial shade and good drainage.
Make sure the bin sits directly on bare soil so worms can arrive.

## What to add

Keep a rough balance between greens and browns.
Greens are fresh and wet, such as vegetable peelings and lawn clippings.
Browns are dry and woody, such as torn cardboard and dead leaves.
Chop large pieces so they rot faster.
Avoid meat, dairy and cooked food.
A handful of garden soil adds helpful microbes.
Water the heap lightly if it feels dry.

## Common problems

If the heap smells bad, it is usually too wet.
Add more browns and turn the pile with a fork.
If nothing seems to happen, it is usually too dry or too cold.
Sprinkle some water and add fresh greens.
Flies are drawn to food scraps left on the surface, so bury them.
