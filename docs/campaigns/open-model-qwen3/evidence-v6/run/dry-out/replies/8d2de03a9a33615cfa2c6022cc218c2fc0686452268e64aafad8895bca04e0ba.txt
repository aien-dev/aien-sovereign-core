# How to Sharpen a Kitchen Knife by Hand

## Gather Your Tools  
Before you begin, gather the necessary tools to ensure a safe and effective sharpening process. You will need a high-quality whetstone—preferably a natural or synthetic stone with a medium to fine grit, such as 800 or 1000 grit. A honing rod is optional but helpful for maintaining edge alignment. A clean, dry towel or cloth is essential for wiping the knife after sharpening. Additionally, have a cutting board and a small bowl of water nearby to keep the whetstone moist during use. A ruler or straightedge can help you maintain the correct angle, typically between 15 and 20 degrees, depending on the knife type. Make sure your workspace is clean, well-lit, and free of clutter to avoid accidents.

## Sharpen the Edge  
Place the knife on a stable surface with the blade facing down. Hold the knife at a consistent angle—usually around 15 to 20 degrees—using the ruler or straightedge as a guide. Begin by dragging the blade across the whetstone in a smooth, even motion, starting from the heel of the blade to the tip. Apply light, even pressure and repeat the motion 5 to 10 times on each side. After a few strokes, switch to a finer grit whetstone to refine the edge and remove any burrs. Always maintain the same angle throughout the process to preserve the knife’s integrity. Use a back-and-forth motion to ensure both sides of the blade are evenly sharpened. This step is crucial to ensure the edge is sharp, clean, and capable of cutting through food efficiently.

## Test and Store  
After sharpening, test the knife’s edge by slicing through a piece of paper or a ripe apple. A sharp knife should cut cleanly with minimal resistance. If the blade still feels dull or skips, repeat the sharpening process with a slightly finer whetstone. Once satisfied with the performance, wipe the blade with a dry cloth to remove any residue. Store the knife in a knife block or on a magnetic strip to prevent damage and maintain its edge. Regular sharpening helps extend the life of your kitchen knife and ensures better performance in daily cooking. Remember, maintaining the correct angle and using a quality whetstone are key to achieving a long-lasting, sharp edge.
