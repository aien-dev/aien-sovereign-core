A savings account is a simple way to store money safely.  
When you deposit money into your savings account, it goes into a balance that grows over time.  
Deposits can be made in cash or through a bank transfer.  
The bank pays you interest on your balance, which means you earn money just for keeping your funds in the account.  
Interest is usually small but adds up over time, helping your savings grow.  
You can check your balance at any time through your bank app or online portal.  
Balances are updated automatically after every deposit or withdrawal.  
Withdrawing money from your savings account means taking funds out of your balance.  
You can withdraw money for emergencies, big purchases, or other needs.  
Withdrawals are limited by your bank’s rules to avoid overspending.  
Interest rates vary by bank and account type, so it's good to compare offers.  
The interest you earn is calculated on your current balance, not the original amount.  
If you withdraw money, your balance decreases, and interest on the remaining balance continues.  
Balancing your savings helps you plan for future goals like buying a car or a house.  
Deposits and withdrawals are recorded in your account history, which you can review anytime.  
Interest is typically paid monthly or annually, depending on the bank.  
Balancing your savings with your spending habits ensures financial stability.  
You can set up automatic deposits to build your balance over time.  
Understanding how deposits, withdrawals, and interest work helps you manage your money wisely.  
Balances are always accurate and reflect your current financial situation.
