# How to Sharpen a Kitchen Knife by Hand

## Gather Your Tools  
Before you begin, gather the necessary tools to ensure a safe and effective sharpening process. You will need a high-quality whetstone—preferably a natural or synthetic stone with a medium to fine grit, such as 800 or 1000 grit. A honing rod is optional but helpful for maintaining edge alignment. A clean, dry towel or cloth is essential for wiping the knife after sharpening. Additionally, have a cutting board and a small bowl of water nearby to keep the whetstone moist during use. A ruler or straightedge can help you maintain the correct angle, typically between 15 and 20 degrees, depending on the knife type. Make sure your workspace is clean, well-lit, and free of clutter to avoid accidents.

## Sharpen the Edge  
Place the knife on a stable surface with the bevel facing down. Hold the knife firmly with your non-dominant hand, and use your dominant hand to guide it across the whetstone at a consistent angle. Begin with the coarse side of the whetstone to remove any nicks or burrs, then progress to the finer side for a smooth, sharp edge. Always move the knife in a straight, even motion, applying light, even pressure. Maintain a consistent angle—around 15 to 20 degrees—using the ruler or straightedge as a guide. Repeat this motion 5 to 10 times on each side of the blade. After each pass, wipe the blade with a dry cloth to remove debris and ensure the edge is clean. The whetstone should feel slightly warm after several passes, which indicates effective sharpening. Avoid pressing too hard, as this can cause the blade to dull or chip.

## Test and Store  
After sharpening, test the knife’s edge by gently slicing through a piece of paper or a thin apple slice. A sharp knife should cut cleanly with minimal resistance. If the blade still feels dull or skips, repeat the sharpening process. Once satisfied, clean the knife with warm, soapy water and dry it thoroughly. Store the knife in a knife block, sheath, or on a magnetic strip to prevent damage. Always keep the blade dry and avoid stacking knives directly on top of each other. Regular sharpening helps maintain the knife’s performance and extends its lifespan. Remember, a well-maintained edge not only improves cooking efficiency but also enhances safety in the kitchen. Proper angle and consistent whetstone use are key to achieving a long-lasting, sharp blade.
