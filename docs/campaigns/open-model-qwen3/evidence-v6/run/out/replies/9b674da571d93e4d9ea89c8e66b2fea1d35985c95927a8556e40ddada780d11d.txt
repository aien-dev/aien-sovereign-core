A savings account is a simple way to store money safely and grow it over time.  
When you deposit money into a savings account, the bank records that amount as your balance.  
Deposits can be made in cash, checks, or through online banking.  
The balance in your account shows how much money you currently have available.  
Interest is a small amount of money the bank pays you for keeping your funds in the account.  
Interest is usually earned monthly and added to your balance automatically.  
The interest rate varies by bank and account type, and it's typically low.  
You can check your interest earnings by reviewing your account statement.  
Withdrawing money from your savings account means taking out a portion of your balance.  
Withdrawals can be done in person, by phone, or through online banking.  
You may need to use a PIN or provide identification to withdraw.  
Balancing your account means keeping track of deposits and withdrawals to ensure accuracy.  
Balancing helps you avoid overdrafts and ensures your records match your actual funds.  
Interest is not guaranteed and may change based on market conditions.  
You can withdraw money at any time, but frequent withdrawals may reduce your interest earnings.  
The balance is updated every time you deposit or withdraw.  
Interest is calculated on the balance, so more money means more interest over time.  
Balancing your account regularly helps you stay informed about your financial health.  
Deposits and withdrawals are recorded in your transaction history.  
Understanding how interest, deposits, withdrawals, and balances work helps you manage your money wisely.
